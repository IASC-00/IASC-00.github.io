      See it before you hear it

      Lesson one is on a laptop with free DJ software, so you can see the beats line up on screen. No gear needed yet.

      Before then, try the practice tool in your browser: move the tempo and nudge one deck until its beats line up with the other. Then the phrasing tool: count bars and phrases, and start the next song on the phrase.

    Starter gear

    You don't need to buy anything before lesson one. When you're ready, here's what's worth getting.

    LessonsStudent pathStarter gearWeekend videosStudents' nightPractice tool

    Lesson one

    Free software, your laptop

    Serato DJ Lite is free and runs on both controllers below, but it cannot record a mix. To record practice mixes for feedback, use rekordbox Free with the DDJ-FLX4 (the same software club DJs use to prep USB sticks), or Mixxx, which is free, records, and runs on older laptops.

    No gear at all yet? Start with the practice tool.

    Main pick

    Pioneer DJ DDJ-FLX4

    Two decks with the same layout as club gear, one USB-C cable. Works with Serato DJ Lite and rekordbox, so it carries you from laptop lessons to USB play.

    Budget pick

    Numark Mixtrack Platinum FX

    Lower cost, big jog wheels with displays, Serato DJ Lite included. Lots of buttons, which can feel like a lot on day one.

    Maker's page

    Headphones

    Closed-back, over-ear

    Pioneer HDJ-X5 (made for DJs) or Audio-Technica ATH-M40x (a good all-rounder). Closed-back so you can hear your cue over the room.

    Save money

    Borrow or buy used

    Used controllers from music stores and resale sites often sell well under new. Chicago rentals are priced by the day, so they rarely make sense for a week or two.
